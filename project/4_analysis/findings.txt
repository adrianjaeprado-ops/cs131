=== Phase 4 - Findings ===

Data source: gs://adrianjaeprado-cs131-backblaze/output/failure_rate_by_model
(output of failure_analysis.py, run on Dataproc)

We limited our comparison to drive models with at least 100,000 drive-days,
since models with only a handful of observations could show a misleadingly
high or low failure rate just from one or two incidents. Once we did that,
WDC WUH722222ALE6L4 came out on top as the most reliable drive in our fleet,
failing only 0.0010% of the time across 11.5 million drive-days. That's
notable because it's also the model that became the most common drive in
our dataset by Q1 2026, so a low failure rate paired with heavy deployment
is a good sign. TOSHIBA MG08ACA16TEY sat at the opposite end, with a 0.0217%
failure rate, more than 20 times higher than WDC's despite having a similar
number of drive-days to compare against.

We were also curious whether a drive's age had much to do with how often it
failed, so we looked at each model's failure rate next to its average
power-on-hours (SMART 9), which tells us how long a drive has actually been
running rather than how old it is by calendar date. There was a
relationship, but it wasn't especially strong, landing around a correlation
of 0.393. A few of the drives with the highest average running hours, like
HGST HMS5C4040BLE640 at over 76,000 hours, still had low failure rates,
while some drives with far less runtime failed more often. That tells us
manufacturer and model design probably matter just as much as, if not more
than, how long a drive has been in use.

Takeaway: reliability seems to come down to a mix of factors, including
manufacturer, model generation, and usage, rather than age by itself.

Methodology: Analysis limited to drive models with 100,000+ drive-days to
avoid single-failure outliers skewing the ranking. 33 of 80 total models met
this threshold, representing the vast majority of total drive-days in the
dataset.

Limitations: Power-on-hours is a proxy for usage, not true drive age; future
work could incorporate specific SMART error counters (5, 187, 197, 198)
alongside age for a fuller failure model.
